# Promise

State the edit in one sentence before you choose a shot.

This edit will make the viewer feel, understand, believe, or do ___ by arranging ___ into ___ over ___ seconds.

Lock these, then stop planning and cut:

- What it is: explainer, product, ad, trailer, documentary, or music.
- What the viewer should do: keep watching, understand, trust a claim, feel the stakes, or remember.
- The curve: curiosity to clarity, problem to solution, calm authority, escalating energy, or discovery.
- The time they asked for, and the project frame unless they asked for another.

"Make it snappier" means an observable change: a shorter shot, an earlier hook, a repeated idea removed, a tighter action, or a different order. It does not mean a zoom on every shot.

Judge a cut in this order. The higher one wins.

1. Viewer effect. What should they feel or ask at this instant?
2. Story. Does the cut advance the promise, the reveal, the proof, or the payoff?
3. Attention. Does the eye land where the next shot needs it?
4. Rhythm. Does the cut meet breath, speech, movement, or a beat, or is the break intentional?
5. Continuity. Are time, space, direction, and cause clear enough for this piece?
6. Cleanliness. Are the frames, the audio join, and the captions free of a defect you can avoid?

If two of these conflict, name the tradeoff and keep the higher one.
