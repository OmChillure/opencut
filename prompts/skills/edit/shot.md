# Shot and cut

Every shot earns its time with one job: orient, reveal, prove, compare, intensify, relieve, bridge, humanize, or close. A beautiful frame with no job is cut. A montage needs an argument. Many cool shots are not yet a montage.

Cut at the earliest frame where this shot has delivered its job and the next shot can carry the viewer. Do not wait for the file to end. For dialogue, cut on a change of thought, not on every sentence. For action, cut just before or during the decisive movement so the next angle finishes it. For a face, hold until it can be read, then cut on a breath or a changed thought. If you cannot name why the cut happens, the order is wrong. A transition will not fix that.

Shot jobs:

- Orient: where, and at what scale.
- Insert: the one detail that matters.
- Reaction: what the face knows that the action did not say.
- Cutaway: compresses time, covers a jump, or adds a fact. Prefer one that adds meaning.
- Proof: the claim becomes visible.
- Bridge: two moments that do not match can still connect.
- Relieve: a breath before the density rises.
- Close: the image or the sound that seals the beat.

Continuity, when the viewer must believe the moments connect:

- Screen direction. After `l2r`, the next shot is `l2r` or `none`. Cut to `r2l` only when the idea reverses. The same rule runs the other way.
- Eyeline. A look to screen right is answered by the thing they are looking at.
- Match on action. Cut during the movement and continue it in the next angle.
- The object stays the object: color, logo, screen, and what the hands are holding.
- Scale. Do not cut from a detail to a wide if the viewer cannot tell what was magnified.
- Sound. Room and bed should not jump unless the place changed.

When continuity fails, pick one: cover it with another imported angle, motivate it as a time jump, or separate it with a title or a held sound. Do not generate a new shot to hide it unless they asked for one.

Montage, only when the sequence is the idea:

- Accumulation, until the claim feels proven.
- Compression: drop the procedure, keep the cause and the result.
- Contrast: before and after, promise and reality.
- A motif that returns with a changed meaning.

Refuse random B-roll under the words, a picture that only restates the sentence, a speed ramp with no action, and escalation that arrives before the stakes exist.

On the plan: `source_in` and `duration` are the job, not the file. `cover` only on a jump. Omit `transition` for a hard cut, another angle of the same moment, or a matched action. `dissolve` when time or place changes. A sound cross is not a slot field.
